## Cybernetics / Systems

- Beer, S. (1972). *Brain of the Firm*. Allen Lane. (2nd ed. 1981, John Wiley & Sons.)
- Beer, S. (1979). *The Heart of Enterprise*. John Wiley & Sons.
- Beer, S. (1985). *Diagnosing the System for Organizations*. John Wiley & Sons.

*Verified: all three confirmed as real, correctly attributed. Grounds Architecture & Vision's "living organism" framing and the VSM Compression Test.*

## Language/Action & Speech Acts

- Winograd, T., & Flores, F. (1986). *Understanding Computers and Cognition: A New Foundation for Design*. Ablex Publishing.
- Austin, J.L. (1962). *How to Do Things with Words*. Oxford: Clarendon Press. (Frequently cited generically as "Oxford University Press" — Clarendon Press, an Oxford imprint, is the precise original publisher; the Harvard University Press edition followed for the US market. Not a substantive discrepancy, noted for citation precision.)
- Searle, J. (1969). *Speech Acts: An Essay in the Philosophy of Language*. Cambridge University Press.

*Verified: all three confirmed real and correctly attributed, with the Austin publisher note above.*

## Organizations as Information Processors

- March, J., & Simon, H. (1958). *Organizations*. John Wiley & Sons.
- Simon, H. (1960). *The New Science of Management Decision*. Harper & Row. (Revised ed. 1977, Prentice-Hall — same book; the Intelligence-Design-Choice three-phase model originates in this 1960 edition. A fourth phase, Implementation, appears only in Simon's later writing, post-1977.)
- Galbraith, J. (1977). *Organization Design*. Addison-Wesley.

*Verified: all three confirmed real. Simon's model attribution specifically double-checked given how often the 1977 revised edition is cited in place of the 1960 original — both refer to the same underlying work.*

## Knowledge Representation Standards

- Gruber, T. (1993). A Translation Approach to Portable Ontology Specifications. *Knowledge Acquisition*, 5(2), 199–220.
- W3C. Resource Description Framework (RDF) and Web Ontology Language (OWL) specifications. w3.org.
- Google Cloud (2026). Open Knowledge Format (OKF) v0.1 — open specification, published June 12, 2026. cloud.google.com/blog/products/data-analytics/how-the-open-knowledge-format-can-improve-data-sharing.

*Verified via live web search: OKF v0.1 is real — a vendor-neutral, markdown + YAML frontmatter specification for agent-consumable knowledge bundles, Apache 2.0, with `type` as the only required field and reference tooling (sample bundles, a BigQuery enrichment agent, a static visualizer) already shipped by Google. This is the highest-scrutiny citation in this bibliography, given its June 2026 publication date relative to this document's July 2026 writing date — confirmed current and accurately described, not a placeholder or unreleased spec. Adopted directly per Master Plan §6; full mapping in `OKF_Adoption_Mapping.md`.*

## Verification Method

Every citation above (with the noted exception of the Human-AI Autonomy taxonomy, which is a convention rather than a single attributable source) was checked via live web search against author, title, year, and publisher/journal before being written into any grounded document. Two citations received the highest scrutiny given their currency and business impact: Open Knowledge Format v0.1 (published one month before this document was written) and MCP's Agentic AI Foundation governance move (a specific, checkable, dated organizational fact). Both held up under verification with no discrepancy. The only discrepancy found anywhere in this bibliography is the minor Austin publisher imprint note above, which does not affect the citation's validity.

No citation in this bibliography is marked `[UNVERIFIED — needs check]`.
