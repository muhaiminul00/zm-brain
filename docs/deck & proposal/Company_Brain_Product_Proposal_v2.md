# Company Brain
### Captures what your company knows — so nobody has to explain it twice. Not your team, not your AI.

---

## The Problem

Ask your best salesperson why a deal fell apart eight months ago. They'll remember. Ask your CRM. It won't.

That's the pattern everywhere: the reason behind a decision, the thing a customer was promised, the real reason a process works the weird way it works — all of it lives in someone's head, in a Slack thread nobody will scroll back to, in a meeting nobody wrote down. It's not lost. It's just not anywhere you can get to it when you actually need it.

AI makes this worse, not better. Every new chat starts from zero. You paste the same context in again. It gives you a confident, wrong answer because it never had the full picture to begin with — it just sounds like it did.

## Why Now

Every serious tech company is racing to solve this right now, and that's not a coincidence. AI agents finally got good enough to do real work inside a company — but only as good as what they're told. Y Combinator named this exact problem a priority category this year: "Company Brain." Half a dozen well-funded teams are building toward it. That's not a warning sign for us — it's a sign the pain is real and the timing is right.

## What We're Building

Company Brain connects to the tools your team already uses and quietly turns everything that happens — decisions, conversations, commitments, work — into one shared, living memory. Not a place you have to go read. Something that follows you and your AI into whatever you're already doing.

So the next time someone asks:

- *Why did we choose this vendor?*
- *What did we tell this customer last time?*
- *Has this bug come up before, and how did we fix it?*
- *What did we actually agree to on that call?*

The answer is already there. No searching. No asking around. No re-explaining it for the fourth time this month.

## What Makes Us Different

A handful of other teams are building toward the same idea right now — and honestly, that's a good sign, it means we're not imagining the problem. But almost all of them stop in the same place: pull everything in, make it searchable, hand it to an AI. That's half the job.

The other half is trust. Before an AI agent acts on a piece of company memory, it needs to know: where did this come from, is it still true, and is this agent even allowed to act on it? Most of the current crop skips that part entirely. We built it first — not as an afterthought bolted on later, but as the foundation everything else sits on.

## What It's Not

- **Not a wiki.** Nobody has to remember to write anything down.
- **Not search.** Search hands you a document. This hands you an answer.
- **Not a smarter chatbot.** It's what sits underneath every conversation — not one more conversation to have.

## Where We Are Today

Straight talk: we've done the deep thinking, not the building yet. How memory should work, how trust should work, how this plugs cleanly into the AI tools people already use every day — that part is done, and we're confident in it. What doesn't exist yet is the product in your hands.

We're talking to people before we build further, because we'd rather find out now whether this is a problem you actually have — not after we've spent months building the wrong thing.
